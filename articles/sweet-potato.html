Sweet potatoes: the orange superfood

From the fields of Samkura Bio Farm, Tsnori

← Back to the farm

Sweet potatoes are our main crop. People who try them come back for more. They're sweet, filling and easy to cook, and few vegetables are as good for you.

Not a potato at all

Sweet potatoes (Ipomoea batatas, ბატატი) look like potatoes, but the two plants aren't closely related. Sweet potatoes belong to the morning-glory family and come from Central and South America. They grow well in warm places with long summers, and that's why they do so well here in Kakheti.

Five reasons to eat more of them

1. Vitamin A for your eyes, skin and immunity

The orange colour comes from beta-carotene, which your body turns into vitamin A as it needs it. One medium sweet potato covers more than a whole day's vitamin A. Vitamin A helps you see in low light, keeps skin and the linings of your body healthy, and supports your immune system. Eat your sweet potato with a little fat, such as olive oil, butter or walnuts, because that helps your body absorb beta-carotene.

2. Fibre for a happy gut

Sweet potatoes give you both soluble and insoluble fibre, and much of it is in the skin. Fibre keeps digestion regular, feeds the good bacteria in your gut and keeps you full for longer. Our skins are clean and grown without synthetic chemicals, so you can scrub them and eat them.

3. Steady energy

Sweet potatoes are naturally sweet, but their fibre slows down how fast the sugar reaches your blood. How you cook them matters: boiled sweet potatoes have a much lower glycaemic index than baked or roasted ones. If you watch your blood sugar, boil or steam them and eat them with some protein.

4. Potassium for your heart

Potassium helps balance the effect of salt and supports normal blood pressure. It also helps your muscles and nerves work properly. Sweet potatoes are a good, tasty source of it.

5. Antioxidants

Beta-carotene, vitamin C and the polyphenols in the skin are all antioxidants. They help protect your cells from oxidative stress. Purple-fleshed varieties also contain anthocyanins, the same pigments that colour blueberries.

“We dig our sweet potatoes by hand in autumn, then let them rest in a warm place for a while. That rest is called curing, and it makes them much sweeter.”

How to store them

  • Keep them in a cool, dark, dry place, ideally 12–15 °C, such as a pantry or cellar.
  • Don't refrigerate raw sweet potatoes. The cold makes the centre hard and changes the taste.
  • Stored well, cured sweet potatoes keep for weeks or even months.

Simple ways to cook them

  • Roasted wedges: leave the skin on, toss with olive oil, salt and a pinch of svanuri marili, then roast at 200 °C for 30–35 minutes.
  • Baked whole: prick with a fork and bake for about 45–60 minutes until soft. Top with matsoni and herbs.
  • Mash: boil, then mash with butter and a little garlic. It's a great side with mtsvadi.
  • Soup: blend with onion, carrot and pumpkin for a velvety autumn soup.
  • Sweet: bake with walnuts and honey for an easy Georgian-style dessert.
