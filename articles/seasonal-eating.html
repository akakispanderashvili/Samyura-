Eating with the Kakheti seasons

What's ripe, and when, at Samkura Bio Farm

← Back to the farm

A tomato in August and a tomato in February are two different foods. Eating with the seasons is the simplest way to get the best taste and the most nutrients.

Why seasonal is better

Food in season ripens naturally on the plant and reaches you within days. Vitamins such as vitamin C slowly break down after harvest, so fresher food has more of them. It's also cheaper, and it needs no heated greenhouses or long trips.

Our year at a glance

SeasonWhat's ripe
Spring (Mar–May)Fresh greens & herbs, sweet potato planting
Early summer (Jun)Cucumbers, first tomatoes
High summer (Jul–Aug)Tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, peaches
Autumn (Sep–Nov)Sweet potatoes, pumpkins, grapes, late peppers
Winter (Dec–Feb)Stored sweet potatoes & pumpkins

Summer: tomatoes, peppers & eggplant

Tomatoes are rich in lycopene, an antioxidant linked to heart health. Your body absorbs more of it when tomatoes are cooked with a little oil. Red bell peppers contain more vitamin C than oranges. Eggplant brings fibre, and its purple skin holds an antioxidant called nasunin. Put them all in a pot and you have ajapsandali.

Summer fruit: peaches

Peaches picked ripe are a completely different fruit from supermarket peaches. They give you vitamin C, potassium and polyphenols. Eat them the same day, or keep them at room temperature until they smell sweet.

Autumn: the big harvest

Autumn is our busiest time. We dig the sweet potatoes, pick the pumpkins, and all of Kakheti harvests grapes for rtveli. Sweet potatoes and pumpkins are both full of beta-carotene, which is exactly what you need going into winter.

Winter: stored goodness

Cured sweet potatoes keep for months in a cool, dark place, so we have sweet potatoes for you all winter. Soups, roasts and mashes keep the farm on your table until spring.
