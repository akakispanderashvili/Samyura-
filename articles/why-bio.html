Why choose bio?

What organic farming changes, and what it doesn't

← Back to the farm

“Bio” and “organic” mean the same thing: food grown without synthetic pesticides, herbicides or chemical fertilisers. Here's why it matters to us, and why it might matter to you.

What bio farming means

Organic farmers keep plants healthy by keeping the soil healthy, not by spraying chemicals. In practice, that means:

  • Compost and manure instead of synthetic fertilisers
  • Crop rotation, so pests and diseases don't build up in one place
  • Weeding by hand or machine instead of herbicides
  • Natural pest control, such as beneficial insects, companion plants and physical barriers
  • No GMOs

What the research says

Fewer pesticide residues

The clearest difference is on your plate. Large reviews of the research keep finding that organic produce has far fewer pesticide residues than conventional produce. That matters most for children and during pregnancy.

More antioxidants, often

A large 2014 meta-analysis in the British Journal of Nutrition found that organic crops had higher levels of many antioxidant compounds, such as polyphenols, than conventional crops. Vitamin and mineral levels are usually similar. When it comes to vitamins, freshness and ripeness count for more than the farming method.

Better for soil, water and wildlife

Organic farms tend to have more earthworms, insects, birds and pollinators. When no synthetic chemicals are used, none of them run off into rivers. For us, that river is the Alazani.

Bio food isn't magic. It's food grown without shortcuts, by people who plan to farm the same soil for the rest of their lives.

Why local matters too

Produce from big supermarkets is often picked unripe so it survives a long trip. Our produce goes from the field in Tsnori to your kitchen in days, so we can let it ripen fully on the plant. Ripe produce tastes better, and you can taste it in a tomato.

How to tell real bio produce

  • Ask the farmer how they grow. A real bio farmer is happy to tell you.
  • Look for an official organic certificate where one exists.
  • Don't expect perfect shapes. Real produce comes in all sizes, with the odd mark from the soil.
  • Buy what's in season. Bio tomatoes in January are a warning sign.
